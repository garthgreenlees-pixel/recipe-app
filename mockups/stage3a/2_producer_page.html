The reputation — the story that sells at the table

The roastery sits where the mountain road flattens out, two blocks from the Wednesday farmers' market, and the queue on Saturday mornings tells you most of what you need to know. The roaster came up through restaurant kitchens before the roasting machine, and it shows: the cupping table is run like a pass, the curves are logged like fridge temps, and nothing ships that wouldn't be served.

The house style is clarity over darkness — washed Ethiopias that read as jasmine and lemon peel, a Huila that holds its sweetness into the cup's last third. Six farm relationships are named on the bags, visited on a rota, paid above C-price, and the roastery publishes what it pays. This is the register the Library trusts: the provenance is on the label because it is real.
