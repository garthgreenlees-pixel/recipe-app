Pacific Coast of Mexico — Sinaloa · Nayarit · Jalisco

Ceviche

● White-fleshed fish, 1.5cm dice ▾
The acid does not cook the fish — it denatures it. Lime juice at pH 2.0–2.5 breaks the surface proteins to opaque and firm within 10–12 minutes. The cut matters: 1.5cm exposes enough surface for the cure without overcooking the interior. Beyond 30 minutes, the fish becomes rubbery and the acid flavour turns sharp.
● Mexican Key lime, freshly squeezed ▾
Never pre-squeezed. Enzymatic degradation begins within 20 minutes of cutting — the brightness fades, the bite turns flat. Squeeze to order, cure to order. Key lime has a more floral, less aggressive acidity than Persian lime; this is not interchangeable in the Mexican tradition.
● Jalapeño, seeded ▾
Mexican ceviche uses jalapeño rather than the ají amarillo of Peruvian tradition — producing a greener, fresher heat profile without the fruity complexity of Andean chillies. Remove seeds and membrane for controlled heat. Leave both for full intensity. Add at the last moment — jalapeño continues to release heat as it sits in the acid.
● Plum tomato, 5mm dice, drained ▾
The defining mark of Mexican (not Peruvian) ceviche. Tomato adds body and natural sweetness that balances the acid. Dice small — tomato must not overwhelm the fish. Drain excess water before adding; undrained tomato dilutes the tiger's milk and weakens the cure.
● Fresh coriander, bruised ▾
Added last, just before service. Bruise gently rather than chopping finely — the essential oils release slowly through the eating window rather than all at once. Finely chopped coriander oxidises quickly and turns bitter. Whole leaves and torn sprigs hold through the 30-minute service window.
