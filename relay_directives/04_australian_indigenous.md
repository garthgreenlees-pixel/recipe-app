## Cultural Care — Highest Standard in the Queue

This canon requires the highest cultural care in the entire queue. The following rules are non-negotiable and override any general efficiency consideration:

1. **Attribution to specific peoples and Country.** Every entry names the specific people(s) and Country (not "Aboriginal Australia" as a single entity). If a source attributes a practice to a specific people (e.g., Yolŋu, Noongar, Anangu, Wiradjuri), that attribution is preserved exactly as the source gives it.
2. **Published, recognized authorities only.** Anchor only to sources that are published, have named authors or institutional backing, and are web-verifiable. No oral-tradition summaries without an intermediary published source.
3. **Restricted knowledge stays out.** If a source indicates that knowledge is culturally restricted, sacred, or not for general circulation — it does not go in the database. Absence over appropriation, without exception.
4. **No pan-Aboriginal flattening.** "Aboriginal Australians" is not a single culinary tradition. Coastal Yolŋu practice is distinct from desert Anangu practice is distinct from south-eastern peoples' practice. Each entry carries the specific attribution its source supports.

## Territory

### Native Larder — Species Precision Required
- Native fruits: quandong, Kakadu plum (gubinge / murunga), Davidson's plum, finger lime, bush tomato (akudjura), muntries — each is a separate entry; species name and country of origin within Australia required
- Seeds and grains: wattleseed preparation traditions (grinding, roasting, leaching), native millet, pigweed seed — document the specific preparation method, not just the ingredient
- Leaves and roots: warrigal greens (oxalic acid management — a genuine technique entry), bush onion, various yam species with specific preparation notes
- Proteins: goanna, kangaroo, emu — preparation traditions where documented in recognized sources with specific attribution; witchetty grub preparation (documented and widely published — note the specific source)

### Earth-Oven and Ground-Cooking Traditions
- Ground-oven techniques — name the specific people and region; cross-reference with PMT earth-oven lineage (hāngi / umu) only where a source explicitly makes this connection
- Hot-stone cooking variants — document regional distinctions
- Ash-roasting and ember techniques — specific to peoples and Country

### Seed and Grain Traditions
- Grinding and stone-milling traditions — specific peoples, specific grains
- Leaching and detoxification techniques (e.g., cycad nut preparation — a technically complex process that is well documented and important to document accurately)
- Fermentation where documented in recognized sources

### Coastal and Riverine Practice
- Trap and weir systems (e.g., Budj Bim eel traps — UNESCO World Heritage, well-documented, recognized authority sources exist)
- Smoking and drying of fish — specific peoples, specific methods
- Shellfish preparation — specific coastal peoples, midden evidence where cited from archaeological sources

### Contemporary Documented Expression
- The contemporary Indigenous Australian culinary expression (Bruce Pascoe, Mindy Woods, Mark Olive, and other recognized contemporary practitioners — web-verify each)
- This layer documents how traditional knowledge is expressed in recognized contemporary form; it does not replace traditional attribution

### PMT Facets
Where a technique connects to the Pacific Migration Trail, note the facet in the `pmt_facet` field with the connecting node named. Do not assume PMT connection — only note where a source establishes it.

## Source Anchoring

Web-verify all authorities before anchoring. Candidates:

- **Bruce Pascoe** — *Dark Emu* (verify edition, publisher, year — note the scholarly debate around some claims; use only what is well-corroborated by archaeological or botanical sources)
- **CSIRO and Australian National Herbarium** species databases (public domain, web-verifiable) for native ingredient species precision
- **Australian Institute of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Studies (AIATSIS)** resources where publicly available
- **Mindy Woods** and other recognized contemporary Indigenous Australian chefs with published work (web-verify each)
- **State herbaria** (RBGSYD, MEL, etc.) for native plant identification

If a source cannot be web-verified as a recognized authority, note the gap and proceed with verified sources only.

## Language

Retain language-group terms (Yolŋu Matha, Noongar, Pitjantjatjara, etc.) on first use with English translation. Note the language group. Do not standardize spelling across language groups — use the spelling from the source.
