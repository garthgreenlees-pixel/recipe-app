## Territory

### Adobo as Technique Family
Adobo is not a single dish — it is a technique family defined by the vinegar-salt-fat-time mechanism. Each significant regional variant is a separate entry:

- **Adobo sa gata** (coconut-cream finish) — Bicolano expression
- **Adobo sa dilaw** (turmeric-forward, no soy) — distinct from canonical Manila soy-vinegar
- **Ilocano adobo** — dry-rendered, minimal liquid, specific fat doctrine
- **Kapampangan adobo** — pork fat–rendered, specific vinegar doctrine (often coconut vinegar)
- **Adobong puti** (white adobo, no soy) — technique distinction, not just a colour descriptor
- Canonical Manila adobo as the baseline technique anchor, with the mechanism (acid denaturation + fat rendering + reduction) documented fully

Do not consolidate regional variants. The mechanism differences are the entries.

### Kinilaw
- Kinilaw as a technique family (acid denaturation of protein without heat) — distinguished from ceviche by the souring agents, the timing doctrine, and the fat component
- Specific agents: sukang tuba (coconut vinegar), sukang Iloko (cane vinegar), calamansi — each changes the technique and must be named
- Regional variants: Visayan, Davao, Maranao — named as themselves
- The distinction between kinilaw (technique) and kilawin (offal preparations using the same acid mechanism) documented

### Fermented Larder — Three Separate Entry Families
- **Bagoong** (fermented shrimp paste / fish paste): bagoong alamang (shrimp) vs. bagoong monamon (fish) are different techniques and different culinary roles; the maturation stages (pink, brown) are technique distinctions
- **Patis** (fish sauce): fermentation timeline, the draw-off process, regional producers and traditions where documented
- **Buro** (fermented rice with fish): Kapampangan burong dalag (mudfish), burong hipon (shrimp) — the lactic fermentation mechanism, the rice's role as fermentation substrate, the maturation signal

### Regional Traditions — Named as Themselves
- **Ilocano**: the lean larder, vegetable-forward tradition, pinakbet (the technique, not just the dish — bitter melon's bitterness as an intentional flavour component), bagoong usage doctrine
- **Bicolano**: the Bicolano chile-heat doctrine (not "spicy Filipino food" — Bicol's chile intensity is specific and regionally located), coconut cream as structural fat not garnish, Bicol express as technique illustration
- **Visayan**: the sour-forward tradition (paksiw as technique family, not single dish), seafood precision, lechon tradition specific to Cebu
- **Kapampangan**: considered the Philippines' culinary heartland — the lard doctrine, the pork-fat rendering tradition, sisig (technique: offal transformation via successive heat applications), the morcon family

### Colonial and Trade Threads — Documented, Not Apologized For
- **Spanish-colonial thread**: the estofado and mechado family (braising technique with European structural logic applied to local proteins); adobo's relationship to Spanish escabeche as a preservation mechanism parallel (not origin story — document the parallel without asserting causation unless a source does)
- **Chinese-trade thread**: the pancit family as Chinese noodle technique adapted to Filipino larder; tokwa't baboy (tofu technique); pork lard rendering doctrine (Chinese influence well-documented in Filipino culinary literature)
- **Portuguese Colonial Trail facets**: where genuine PMT-adjacent connections exist (the vinegar-preservation corridor, the escabeche thread), document in the `pmt_facet` field — do not overextend
