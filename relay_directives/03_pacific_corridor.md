## Core Entry Territory

### Earth-Oven Traditions
- `umu` (Samoa/Tonga/Cook Islands) and `lovo` (Fiji/Melanesia) are distinct entries — same physical mechanism, different regional doctrine, different foods, different ceremony. Never flatten into a single "Pacific earth oven" entry.
- Each island group's umu/lovo practice documented separately where sources support it.

### Coconut-Cream Technique
- Extraction method (fresh-scraped vs. dried coconut), cream vs. milk distinction, stage-of-cooking doctrine (added raw vs. reduced vs. tempered), island-specific applications.

### Raw-Fish Preparations
Three separate entries — never merged:
- `ota ika` (Tongan/Samoan raw fish in coconut cream) — Tongan and Samoan variants named as themselves
- `kokoda` (Fijian ceviche-style preparation) — Fijian, not "Pacific ceviche"
- `poisson cru` (Tahitian raw fish in coconut milk and lime) — French Polynesian, with colonial-language term retained as culturally embedded

### Fermentation and Preservation
- Fermented breadfruit (`masi`, `ma`) — island-specific naming
- Coconut toddy and fermented palm products where documented
- Salt-preservation and drying methods (island-specific)

### Island-Specific Larders
- Taro and its preparation variants by island (Samoan vs. Tongan vs. Fijian taro doctrine differs)
- Breadfruit (raw, roasted, fermented, pounded) — each preparation state is a potential entry
- Tropical starches: cassava regional practices, sweet potato (kumara) Pacific corridor expressions distinct from Māori treatment
- Reef and lagoon fish: species precision required — no generic "Pacific fish"

### PMT (Pacific Migration Trail) Facets
Where a technique is a documented waypoint on the PMT (e.g., umu appearing in Hawaii, Māori hāngi, Samoan umu as the same lineage), note the facet connection in the `pmt_facet` field. Do not duplicate the entry — cross-reference by slug.

## Source Anchoring

## Island Specificity Doctrine

A Tongan technique is Tongan. A Fijian technique is Fijian. "Pacific" as a primary label is permitted only for entries that document a shared lineage explicitly across multiple island groups, with each variant named. Default to the most specific attribution the source supports.

## Native Terms

Retain Samoan, Tongan, Fijian, Cook Islands Māori, Tahitian terms on first use with English translation. Subsequent uses may use the term alone. Do not anglicize or paraphrase native technique names.
